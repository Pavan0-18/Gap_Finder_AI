COURSE SYLLABUS: ADVANCED CELLULAR AND MOLECULAR BIOLOGY
Course Code: BIO-301
Academic Term: 2024-2025

UNIT 1: CELLULAR ARCHITECTURE AND DIVISION MECHANICS
Chapter 1: The Eukaryotic Cell Cycle and Mitosis
- Overview of the cell cycle phases: G1, S phase (DNA replication), G2, and M phase.
- Stages of Mitosis: Prophase, Prometaphase, Metaphase, Anaphase, Telophase.
- Spindle apparatus assembly, kinetochore microtubule attachments, and chromosome alignment along the metaphase plate.
- Cytokinesis mechanisms: contractile ring in animal cells versus cell plate formation in plant cells.
- Learning Objectives:
  * Describe the morphological transformations in each phase of mitosis.
  * Contrast cytokinesis mechanisms across animal and plant kingdoms.
  * Identify checkpoints regulating the cell cycle (G1/S, G2/M, spindle assembly).

Chapter 2: Meiotic Division and Sexual Reproduction
- Meiosis I: Prophase I sub-stages (leptotene, zygotene, pachytene, diplotene, diakinesis), synaptonemal complex, and chiasmata.
- Crossing over between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes and genetic recombination.
- Anaphase I segregation: separation of homologous chromosome pairs with sister chromatids remaining joined.
- Meiosis II: separation of sister chromatids resulting in four non-identical haploid gametes.
- Non-disjunction errors and aneuploidy conditions.
- Learning Objectives:
  * Distinguish the chromosome behavior of Meiosis I from that of Mitosis.
  * Explain how crossing over and independent assortment generate genetic diversity.
  * Analyze the phenotypic consequences of meiotic non-disjunction.

UNIT 2: GENETIC INHERITANCE AND MOLECULAR REGULATION
Chapter 3: Mendelian Genetics and Chromosomal Basis of Inheritance
- Monohybrid and dihybrid crosses, law of segregation, law of independent assortment.
- Incomplete dominance, codominance, and sex-linked traits.
- Pedigree analysis and genetic probability calculations.
- Learning Objectives:
  * Solve multigenic inheritance ratios using Punnett squares.
  * Trace X-linked recessive mutations through human pedigrees.
